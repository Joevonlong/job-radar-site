Salary level and salary lookup

A job page has two salary cards under “Pay and benefits”:

  • Salary level (always on, no AI): where the job’s stated salary stands among the salaries of comparable postings you collected, plus links to salary sites.
  • Salary lookup (off by default, uses AI and web search): on request, the AI searches public web pages for the company’s pay level and the band of this role, and shows its sources.

Both are configured in salary.yaml (Settings → Salary, or the Advanced tab).

Salary level: the benchmark

Section titled “Salary level: the benchmark”

After every cycle, the pipeline step rebuilds the benchmark from the postings in your database:

  1. Each posting with a stated salary is converted to a year in the market’s base currency (the same normalized figure the salary card shows). Only postings first seen in the last benchmark.window_days days count.
  2. Its role family comes from the title: the first family in role_families whose pattern matches wins (LLM Engineer → ML / AI engineer). A title that matches no family is not part of the benchmark.
  3. Postings are grouped by family × seniority × place, and each group with at least benchmark.min_sample salaries keeps its percentiles (P10, P25, median, P75, P90).

The job page picks the narrowest group that exists, from “same family, same seniority, same city” through “same country”, “any seniority” to “family only”, and says which one it used and how many salaries it holds. A job with a salary gets its position (“P72: above the median”), a job without one still sees the range of its group. A group built from a handful of postings is a rough hint: the sample size is always shown.

When benchmark.include_ai_lookups is on, finished AI lookups (base salary, confidence medium or high, at least one source on a host of lookup.allowed_fetch_domains, same place) are counted too; a band whose place names only the country counts for the country, not the city; the card then shows the two sample counts separately. It is off by default, so what postings state stays apart from AI estimates.

pnpm salary:benchmark rebuilds the benchmark right away and prints it (--json for the raw groups).

Links to salary sites

Section titled “Links to salary sites”

deep_links lists search pages the card links to, filled in with the job’s company, title or city: Levels.fyi, Glassdoor and Kununu by default. They open in your browser; Job Radar never fetches these sites. The sites block automated clients, so the URL formats were checked by hand; if a site changes its URLs, fix the template in salary.yaml.

Placeholder Becomes
{company} Company name
{company_slug} Company name in lower case, words joined by -
{title} Job title
{city} City
{country} ISO country code

Salary lookup (AI with web search)

Section titled “Salary lookup (AI with web search)”

Turn it on with lookup.enabled: true (Settings → Salary → “Enable salary lookup”). The job page then shows a “Look up salary” button. Nothing is looked up unless you click it.

What happens on a click:

  1. The web app queues a salary_lookup request; the ai-worker (or the runner in container mode) takes it.
  2. The AI is given only the company name, job title, city, country and seniority: never your profile, never the job description. It searches the web, reads salary pages, and answers with the company’s pay level, the band of this role, related roles at the company, a confidence, and the source of every figure.
  3. Job Radar checks the answer before storing it: every figure needs a source link (https) from the answer; figures outside lookup.sanity_annual are dropped; amounts are converted to the base currency (a currency without an exchange rate is shown in its own currency, and figures above lookup.unconvertible_max_annual are dropped); sources on hosts outside lookup.allowed_fetch_domains are marked “other source”. The card says how many items were dropped.
  4. The result is stored and shown for lookup.ttl_days days to every job of the same company, role family, seniority and place without asking again. “Look up again” asks anyway.

Limits:

  • lookup.max_per_day: lookups per 24 hours (every request counts, a failed one too; a cached answer does not). Once they are used up, the button on the job page is disabled until earlier lookups pass 24 hours.
  • lookup.timeout_seconds: one lookup, all providers and retries together.
  • lookup.max_searches: web searches per lookup. The Anthropic API enforces it; the CLIs are asked to keep to it.
  • lookup.web_search_usd: the fee per web search of the Anthropic API, counted in AI usage and the monthly API budget.
  • lookup.allowed_fetch_domains: each entry must name one site. Public suffixes such as co.uk, com.au or github.io are refused, since they would allow every site under them. With an empty list the Anthropic API does not search at all.

Which AI runs it is the task salary_lookup in ai.yaml (Settings → AI → task overrides). Only providers that can search the web are tried:

Provider How it searches Domain limit
claude-cli (default, first) WebSearch and WebFetch tools, pre-approved for the domains in allowed_fetch_domains; needs claude 2.1.296 or newer Pages are fetched only from the listed hosts; search itself is not limited
anthropic (default, second) The API’s server-side web search tool Search results only from the listed domains
codex-cli (only if you add it) Codex’s built-in web search None: the settings page warns about it

DeepSeek and MiMo cannot search the web and are skipped for this task. If no web-capable provider is available, the card says so and links to the AI settings.

Every other AI task still runs without any tools; see ai-providers.md and SECURITY.md.

Reading the result

Section titled “Reading the result”
  • Pay at this company: below / around / above the market, or top of the market, with a typical salary.
  • This role: the band (P10 to P90 style range and median, base or total pay) and, when it is base pay in the base currency, its position in your benchmark group.
  • Related roles at this company: other bands the sources gave.
  • Sources: numbered; every figure refers to them. “Other source” means a host outside your trusted list.

It is an AI estimate from public pages, not a fact of the job. Check the sources before you rely on it.

salary.yaml at a glance

Section titled “salary.yaml at a glance”

Add the words of your market’s language to the family patterns (the shipped file includes German ones). The deep link URLs can be edited on the Settings page too: they are only links your browser opens, and no request of Job Radar goes to them. They must be https:// or http:// URLs.

Troubleshooting

Section titled “Troubleshooting”
The card says What to do
“This title matches no role family” Add a pattern for it to role_families
“Too few postings of this kind state a salary” Fewer than min_sample salaries in every group: wait for more postings, or lower min_sample
“None of the AI providers set for this lookup can search the web” Put claude-cli or anthropic in tasks.salary_lookup.order and make sure it is available (pnpm ai:probe)
auth / rate_limited / timeout The provider’s own problem; the request is not retried automatically
“The ai-worker has not answered” The ai-worker is not running or busy: see /sources
“The salary tables do not exist yet” Run the database migrations (pnpm db:migrate, done by the deploy)
