Does it apply to jobs for me?

Section titled “Does it apply to jobs for me?”

No. Job Radar does not auto-apply, and it never fills in forms on job sites. It finds, reads, scores and keeps track; you decide and you apply. When you want a tailored résumé, it exports the job ad and gives you a command for resume-kit, a separate tool you run yourself (resume-kit.md).

How does scoring work?

Section titled “How does scoring work?”
  1. Hard filters first. Jobs outside your cities, below your salary floor, with a contract type you excluded or from a blocked company are filtered out without any AI call. They aren’t deleted: you can still see them, with the reason.
  2. The AI scores the rest, 0–100. It reads the job ad together with your profile summary, skills, languages, your job preferences and your own scoring criteria (priorities, nice-to-haves, dealbreakers). It returns the score, the reasons the job fits and the gaps. A job that hits one of your dealbreakers lands in the 0–29 band.
  3. Signals add a capped bonus. Your signal categories (a stated salary, equity, benefits, anything you define) add points on top. Rank value = match score + the signal bonus, up to a cap (15 by default). Lists sort by rank value.
  4. Pushes use the rank value. By default a job is pushed at 70 or more, or at 60 with a high-value signal.

When you change your scoring criteria, the current scores are marked stale and scored again over the next cycles. After a profile change, Settings → Manual actions → “Re-score N stale scores” brings the old scores up to date. Details: signals.md, usage.md, telegram.md.

How do I add a company’s career page?

Section titled “How do I add a company’s career page?”

Job Radar reads company career pages hosted on Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Personio and SmartRecruiters.

  • In the browser: Sources → registry (/sources/registry) → add a company: its name, the ATS, and its token (the company’s id on that ATS, usually the last part of its careers page address). “Open board” checks you got it right.
  • In the file: add an entry under ats_boards in data/config/sources.yaml.
  • Let it find boards: with a Tavily key (free plan available), Job Radar searches for new boards about once a day and lists them on the Sources page for you to add or ignore (tavily.md).

The ATS channel itself has to be switched on. More: sources.md.

How do I change the language?

Section titled “How do I change the language?”
  • Interface: the language button in the top bar switches this browser between English and Chinese.
  • Everything else: Settings → Interface → Languages (the file is language.yaml). There you set the default interface language, the language the AI writes in (any language code, such as de or pt-BR), the language job ads are translated into, and the language of Telegram messages and of the command-line tools.

Adding another interface language means adding a messages file. Details: languages.md.

Can I use it on my phone?

Section titled “Can I use it on my phone?”

Yes, two ways:

  • Telegram: the jobs that clear your bar arrive as messages with buttons. Nothing on your machine needs to be reachable from the internet for this.
  • The dashboard: it works at phone width. Open it over Tailscale (tailscale serve --bg 3000), which keeps it inside your own private network.

Don’t forward port 3000 on your router or use a public tunnel: the dashboard has no login unless you set a password, and anyone who reaches it can act as you. If other people share your tailnet, set one with pnpm web:password. Setup: installation.md.

What does it cost?

Section titled “What does it cost?”

Job Radar itself is free and open source (AGPL-3.0), and you run it on your own machine. What can cost money sits outside it:

  • AI: a claude or codex subscription you already have costs nothing extra per job. API keys are billed per token and stop at the monthly budget you set (monthly_api_budget_usd in ai.yaml).
  • Optional sources: Bright Data and Apify (paid fallbacks for LinkedIn) bill your account; JSearch (Google Jobs) and Tavily (finding company boards) have free plans. Each paid or keyed source has a monthly budget.

Without any AI and with only the free sources, it costs nothing beyond your electricity. AI usage and cost are on /sources/ai-usage (ai-usage.md).

Which AI do I need?

Section titled “Which AI do I need?”

None to start: without an AI, Job Radar still collects, de-duplicates, filters and shows jobs. Scoring, translation, job breakdowns and the AI signal check start once you connect one of these:

How
Codex CLI (subscription) codex login on the machine that runs the AI worker
Claude Code (subscription) run claude, then /login
Anthropic, DeepSeek or MiMo (API) the key in .env; DeepSeek and MiMo also need switching on in ai.yaml

By default the worker tries codex, then claude, then the API providers. You can change the order, the model and the effort per task on the AI tab of the Settings page. pnpm ai:probe shows what works right now. More: ai-providers.md.

Will it use up my Claude or ChatGPT plan?

Section titled “Will it use up my Claude or ChatGPT plan?”

It’s built not to. Each subscription CLI has a daily cap (claude 100 calls, codex 200 by default), and claude comes after codex in the order because you probably use claude yourself too. When a provider reports a usage limit, every process pauses it until the stated reset time. Each cycle also has a time budget for AI work (30 minutes by default); what doesn’t fit waits for the next cycle. All of it is adjustable in ai.yaml or on the Settings page (ai-providers.md).

What leaves my machine?

Section titled “What leaves my machine?”

Jobs, your profile and your applications stay in a database on your machine. There are no accounts, no telemetry and no hosted service. What does go out:

  • To the AI you configured: the job text, with your profile summary, skills, languages, job preferences (target roles, cities, salary floor, excluded companies and keywords, contract types) and your scoring criteria, for scoring. Only the job text for translation and breakdowns. Company, title, city, country and seniority when you click “Look up salary”. The jobs you marked and their status, for the weekly suggestions (switch off with suggest.enabled in schedule.yaml).
  • To Telegram: the push messages, to your own bot, if you set one up. They are stored on Telegram’s servers.
  • To the sources you switched on: requests for listings, and checks whether a posting is still online. Search APIs that need a key also see your search terms.

If a provider is unavailable the next one takes over, so every provider you leave switched on can receive the text; switch a provider off to keep text from that vendor (which providers receive job text).

With a subscription CLI, your provider’s consumer terms apply, so check its data and training settings. The résumé hand-off command, when you run it, sends your full résumé and the job ad to Claude.

Nothing in the database is deleted automatically yet: raw postings, emails and notifications stay until you remove the database. The full list of what is stored, each AI provider’s data policy and a checklist to remove everything: privacy.md.

Which job sites does it read?

Section titled “Which job sites does it read?”
Kind Sources Needs In a new install
Job boards, official Arbeitsagentur (German Federal Employment Agency) nothing on (Germany pack)
Job boards, scraped LinkedIn guest search, Indeed, StepStone, XING nothing off: the wizard shows the risk and asks first
Company career pages Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Personio, SmartRecruiters nothing; you add the companies on
Search APIs Google Jobs via JSearch a RapidAPI key (free plan available) off; switch it on once the key is in .env
Your inbox Job-alert emails from a Gmail label a Gmail app password off; switch it on once the password is in .env
LinkedIn, more reliably your own logged-in session (risky, see its guide), Bright Data, Apify a one-time login, or a paid key session off; Bright Data / Apify do nothing without a key

You choose which ones run; one failing never stops the others. A source without its key shows “not configured” and sends nothing. Every source and its states: sources.md.

Is scraping job sites allowed?

Section titled “Is scraping job sites allowed?”

It depends on the site and where you live, and this is not legal advice. Some sources read public pages or unofficial endpoints, and some sites’ terms of service forbid automated access. Using LinkedIn with your own logged-in session breaks LinkedIn’s user agreement and can get your account restricted.

What Job Radar does: it starts with every scraping source switched off and asks you to confirm the risk before you turn one on, it throttles requests, never tries to get past a login wall, CAPTCHA or verification page, and stops using a source that keeps failing. What you do: read the terms of every site you switch on, keep volumes low, prefer official APIs and your own alert emails, and don’t republish what it collects. You are responsible for how you use it.

I’m not in Germany. Does it work for me?

Section titled “I’m not in Germany. Does it work for me?”

Yes. The defaults assume no country, and you pick your market in the setup wizard. A market pack holds what’s specific to a country: its cities and regions, the local-language rules, the base currency and its job boards. Job Radar ships a neutral pack (generic) and a Germany / EU pack (de). For another country, copy a pack into data/config/packs/ and adjust it (market-packs.md). The company career pages, LinkedIn, Indeed and Google Jobs work in many countries; StepStone, XING and Arbeitsagentur are the Germany pack’s own boards.

Salary reading knows the euro, dollar and pound plus many more currencies (among them CAD, AUD, SGD, JPY, CNY, INR, BRL and ZAR), with rough exchange rates you can adjust; the market form tells you when your currency has no rate. In a country Indeed doesn’t cover, only Indeed is skipped and the other sources keep working. Check the salary figures on a few jobs if you’re outside Europe.

Can several people share one installation?

Section titled “Can several people share one installation?”

No. Job Radar is one person, one installation: one profile, one set of preferences, one Telegram chat. Two people means two installations, each with its own database. There are no user accounts inside the app, and none are planned.

Two installations fit on one machine: clone a second checkout and, before its first start, give it its own compose project name, database volume and ports in its .env (COMPOSE_PROJECT_NAME, JOBRADAR_PG_VOLUME, JOB_RADAR_WEB_PORT, DB_HOST_PORT). Each also needs its own Telegram bot. Step by step: multiple-instances.md.

Does my computer have to stay on?

Section titled “Does my computer have to stay on?”

For cycles to run on schedule, yes: Job Radar runs on your machine, not in a cloud. If it was off at a scheduled time, the runner catches up once when it starts again, and takes a missed backup too. To run a Mac unattended (log in after a reboot, start Docker at login, power on after a power cut, no sleep), follow host-setup.md. Pushes need the AI worker to be running as well.

Does it run on Windows?

Section titled “Does it run on Windows?”

Yes, inside WSL2. Clone the repository into the Linux file system (for example ~/job-radar, not under /mnt/c, where bind mounts are slow and permissions differ), switch on Docker Desktop’s WSL integration for that distribution, and install Node, pnpm and the claude / codex CLIs inside WSL. Then follow the Linux steps; the AI worker runs in WSL too, and the dashboard opens at http://127.0.0.1:3000 in the Windows browser. Notes: README, platform notes. Job Radar is run day to day on macOS and Linux, so an issue about anything that behaves differently on WSL is welcome.
